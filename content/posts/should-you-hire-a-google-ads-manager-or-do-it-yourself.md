You've got the budget set aside. You're staring at the **Create Campaign** button. And you're asking yourself the same question every business owner asks at this exact moment: *Can I just do this myself?*

Here's the honest answer: yes, you can. The real question isn't whether you're capable of running Google Ads—it's whether your time is better spent running your business or learning a second one. Let's walk through the actual math, not the sales pitch.

## What DIY Actually Costs You (Hint: It's Not Money)

Running your own Google Ads campaign is free in the sense that you're not paying a management fee. But it isn't free—you're just paying in a different currency: your time.

Published guides commonly put moderate-complexity accounts at roughly [3 to 5 hours a week](https://tryovertime.com/learn/google-adword-management-guide) for ongoing search-term review, bid adjustments, and reporting. If your spend climbs into the thousands each month, that time commitment can rise; one guide estimates accounts near $10,000 in monthly spend may need [8 to 12 hours a week](https://www.kampaio.com/blog/google-ads-without-agency) of manual attention to manage well.

Run the math on your own hourly value and the picture gets clearer fast. If your time is worth $50 an hour to your business, 40 hours a month of DIY ad management represents a $2,000 opportunity cost—whether or not a dollar of it shows up on an invoice.

## What Hiring Actually Costs You

Agency and freelance pricing is all over the map. You'll get a $299 quote and a $3,500 quote for what sounds like the same service, with no easy way to tell what you're actually buying at each price.

A useful rule of thumb: if your monthly ad spend is under roughly $1,500, a traditional agency retainer can feel like a heavy percentage of the budget before a single click happens. Below that level, DIY—or a very lean freelancer arrangement—often makes more sense. Above $2,500 or so in monthly spend, professional management can start pulling its weight: the fee becomes a smaller percentage of a bigger number, and the time you are not spending in the account becomes more valuable.

Before you sign anything, get the full number. One industry article notes that businesses can underestimate their real management costs when setup fees, per-platform charges, and landing-page work sit on top of the quoted monthly rate. Ask for those details upfront, not just the headline percentage.

## The Real Question Isn't Cost. It's Attention.

Here's what almost nobody tells you plainly: a Google Ads account doesn't fail because the platform is broken. It fails because nobody is watching it closely enough, consistently enough, for long enough.

The businesses that lose money on Google Ads aren't always the ones that chose DIY. They're the ones—DIY or hired-out—where the account got set up once and then left alone. A freelancer builds it, hands it over, and nobody logs back in for six weeks. An agency account manager is juggling other clients, and your account gets fifteen minutes of real attention a week. Either way, the result is the same: money leaks out through stale keywords, ignored search terms, and bids nobody adjusted since launch.

So the real question isn't *DIY or hire?* It's: **Who is actually going to look at this account every week, and do they have the time to do it right?**

## When DIY Genuinely Makes Sense

- Your monthly ad spend is under $1,500 and every dollar matters.
- You have a few hours a week you can protect and actually give to this—not squeeze in between everything else.
- You're running one straightforward campaign, not five overlapping campaigns across multiple platforms.
- You're comfortable with a real learning curve and some early trial and error.

## When Hiring Makes Sense

- Your spend is high enough that a management fee is a small fraction of the budget, not a tax on it.
- You genuinely don't have—and won't protect—a few hours a week for this work.
- You're running, or want to run, more than one platform at once.
- You've tried DIY and watched the account drift because life got busy.

## What to Ask Before You Hire Anyone

If you decide to hand this off, ask directly:

- How many other accounts does this person or team manage?
- What does a typical week of attention on my account actually look like?
- What is the full cost, including setup and per-platform fees—not just the headline percentage?
- Who makes the changes, and how will I know what changed and why?

A vague answer to any of those is worth paying attention to.

## The Bottom Line

There's no universally right answer here—there's only the right answer for where your business is right now. DIY isn't beneath you, and hiring help isn't giving up. The only real mistake is choosing either path and then not giving it the attention it needs to work.

## FAQ

### Is $20 a day enough to run Google Ads?

It depends heavily on your industry and cost per click. For a narrow local-service niche, it can work. For competitive industries, it is often too thin to give Google enough room to find your best customers.

### How long before I know if Google Ads is working?

Give a new campaign—DIY or professionally managed—at least 4 to 6 weeks before judging it. Early weeks are naturally noisier while the account and its algorithms are still learning.

### Can I switch from an agency to managing it myself later, or the other way around?

Yes, either direction. Expect a short adjustment period either way. A new set of eyes—yours or a manager's—usually means a few weeks of recalibration before things settle.
